Final recommendation

Don’t use county air quality alone to decide where asthma prevention dollars go. Target by measured asthma burden, and treat clean-air work as protection for people who already have asthma.

  1. Prioritize counties by asthma prevalence and its stronger companions, such as adult smoking and COPD rates, which track asthma far more closely than AQI does (see section 6).
  2. Keep air quality programs, but aim them at exposure: AQI alerts, clean-air days, indoor air filtration and asthma action plans. Judge them by asthma attacks and ER visits, not by prevalence.
  3. Plan high-AQI urban areas like Los Angeles at neighborhood level. County averages hide where pollution and asthma overlap.

This does not mean air pollution is harmless. It is a known asthma trigger. It means a county’s median AQI is too broad a measure to find who has asthma.

Read these results with care

What the data cannot show

  • Cause and effect. This is a one-time, county-level snapshot. It cannot show what any individual breathes or whether pollution causes asthma.
  • Partial-year air data. The typical county has only — days of 2026 AQI readings, and the asthma estimates come from earlier survey years.
  • Modeled asthma rates. PLACES county figures are statistical estimates built from survey and demographic data, not clinic counts, which can smooth out local pollution effects.
  • Only monitored counties. Counties without an EPA monitor are missing, and — monitored counties (—) had no asthma estimate and were excluded.
  • One broad AQI number. Median AQI blends ozone, PM2.5 and other pollutants and hides short, severe pollution days.
  • Name-based merge. A few independent cities and same-name counties (e.g. Baltimore, Fairfax, Roanoke) share one AQI record.

What to analyze next

  • Asthma ER visits and hospitalizations, the outcomes pollution is known to drive, rather than prevalence.
  • Neighborhood (census tract) data in LA County, pairing PLACES tract estimates with local pollution and freeway proximity.
  • Specific pollutants: PM2.5 and ozone days, and full-year or multi-year AQI.
  • Controls for smoking, income and insurance in a multiple regression, to isolate any remaining air quality effect.
  • Childhood asthma, where pollution effects are often strongest.
